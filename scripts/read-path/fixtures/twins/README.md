# The derivations' test fixture

The read-path build derives the occurrences, their identifier candidates, the catalogue's views over the register, the profile pages' links and the Darwin Core relations in DuckDB, ported from Postgres's views (decision 061). These files hold a small set of inputs and the rows each derivation should give from them. The expected rows began as Postgres's own answers, captured on 2026-10-09 before it retired (`salish-9uu.11`), and have been edited since wherever the build was meant to derive something different (below). The directory keeps its old name, `twins`, from when the derivations were checked against Postgres itself.

- **`snapshot/`**: a read-path snapshot, the inputs and the expected rows together, as DuckDB's `EXPORT DATABASE` writes one in CSV, each table's rows sorted. [`twin-fixture.ts`](../../twin-fixture.ts) loads it, moving the register into a file of its own beside the snapshot, where the build keeps it now; [`derive-occurrences.test.ts`](../../derive-occurrences.test.ts) derives from it and checks each derivation against the expected rows beside it. The inputs are the tables the build reads, under their Postgres names: what users write, the source tables the mirrors are written from (`derive/mirrors-from-snapshot.ts`), the register and the catalogue's documents. The expected rows are `snapshot.occurrences`, the link views under `snapshot.`, `snapshot.<view>_answer` for the catalogue's three views, `derived.occurrence_identifier_candidates` and `dwc.*`. The reference tables aren't in it: the test loads them from `data/reference/`, as the build does.
- **`haulout-distance.tsv`**: `public.haulout_distance_m` around three haul-out sites, for `derive/haulout-distance.test.ts`.
- **`ingest-*.json`**: what Postgres's ingest stored for each ingest test's recorded response, for the "mirror stores what Postgres stored" tests.

The smaller derivations' expected answers (the register's fold, for one) are written into their tests.

## Where the rows came from

All of it is synthetic or public. The snapshot was taken (`snapshot.ts --answers`, as of [95c8e13](https://github.com/salish-sea/salishsea-io/blob/95c8e1363b5b42f22c2511161726f275eea35325/scripts/read-path/snapshot.ts)) from a scratch copy of the local database holding, in order:

1. [`supabase/ci-seed.sql`](https://github.com/salish-sea/salishsea-io/blob/386d78df1cdac0b39076d0f7f8a55d55df6f7731/supabase/ci-seed.sql): three Maplify sightings and one native one;
2. register release 2026.10.1, loaded by the [register loader](https://github.com/salish-sea/salishsea-io/blob/5c1497a95301e7cc212e46712705ef5d0f426cec/scripts/register/load.ts);
3. the Bigg's catalogue, from `data/biggs-ids.tsv` by the [catalogue seed](https://github.com/salish-sea/salishsea-io/blob/37564ef580f84996b42c57b907fcb85029c6f984/scripts/seed/seed-biggs.ts), without the withheld notes and stories (rights policy D-21), which the snapshot never read;
4. [`seed.sql`](seed.sql): one or two invented rows per source, so every derivation has something to derive;
5. [`trim.sql`](trim.sql): the catalogue, register, iNaturalist taxa and haul-out sites cut down to what those rows reach, so the fixture can be read. Postgres's answers were computed after it.

Neither SQL file runs any more; they are the record of how the rows were made.

Four input rows were added by hand after Postgres retired, none of which Postgres saw: observations 9000003 and 9000004 in `inaturalist_observations.csv`, and their photos 8000002 and 8000003 in `inaturalist_observation_photos.csv`, whose URLs are iNaturalist's placeholders for a photo flagged for copyright infringement and one flagged as AI-generated. Postgres's answers have neither observation, which is what the build must derive: such a photo takes its observation off the site (decision 081, which replaced `salish-87oq`'s rule of dropping only the photo).

Decision 080's confirmation rule added more, which Postgres never saw either: the `inaturalist.identifications` table, which Postgres's copy didn't hold (`inaturalist_identifications.csv`), with identifications that confirm observation 9000001 two to one though its observer calls it a humpback, leave 9000002 unconfirmed (only a broader taxon, a hidden one, a withdrawn one and one from a deleted account, decision 081, from anyone but its observer) and dispute a new observation, 9000007; and a second bout, `bout_twin0000000000000002`, whose species is tagged only `possible`. The expected occurrences for all of them, and the `confirmation` key on every one, are the build's answer, checked by hand.

iNaturalist's annotations (salish-1y8) came after Postgres too: an `annotations` column on `inaturalist_observations.csv`, where 9000002 carries a Dead annotation that counts and 9000001 an Alive one voted down, which doesn't. The expected occurrences' `vitality` key is the build's answer, checked by hand.

Reading the animals people name in iNaturalist comments and identifications (salish-4s42, decision 106) added `inaturalist_comments.csv` and a `body` column on `inaturalist_identifications.csv`. Observation 9000002, which has no description, gains two identifiers: T137s from its observer's own comment, "T137s (-B)", and T137A from someone else's comment, which names T137B too; the observer's "(-B)" keeps T137B out. An identification's remark names T137s again and adds nothing. A deleted account's comment, a hidden comment and a withdrawn identification's remark each name an animal, and none counts. 9000001's T137A stays its observer's, though a comment names it too, and that comment's J pod joins its pods. The changed document in `snapshot_occurrences.csv`, the new rows in `derived_occurrence_identifier_candidates.csv` and the profile links they make (`snapshot_individual_occurrences.csv`, `snapshot_group_occurrences.csv`, `snapshot_ecotype_occurrences.csv`) are the build's answer, checked by hand.

Showing those comments and identifications on a card (salish-065b, decision 107) added each one's author and date: `created_at` and `name` on `inaturalist_comments.csv`, and `login`, `name` and `created_at` on `inaturalist_identifications.csv`, all invented. A blank `name` is an author with no display name, whom the card credits by login. The expected `activity` and `identified_as` keys of observation 9000002 are the build's answer, checked by hand, and asserted in `derive-occurrences.test.ts` rather than kept in the expected rows, since Postgres never had them.

A matriline's report doesn't reach a member before the member's birth year (salish-fwyt.4.2). To show it, T137A's birth years in `snapshot_individuals.csv` were changed by hand from 2002 to 2027, after every sighting here, so the native sighting's "T137s" no longer links to T137A through its matriline; that row is gone from `snapshot_individual_occurrences.csv`. The birth year is invented, as the other hand edits are.

A matriline's page gathers its sub-lineages' and members' reports (salish-fwyt.7, decision 110). To show a sub-lineage, T065s was added by hand to `snapshot_social_groups.csv` as a matriline (the register in the fixture already had it, as T065As's parent), which adds its rows to the catalogue's answers (`snapshot_group_parents_answer.csv`, `snapshot_matriline_members_answer.csv`); and a user's identification of T065As on `maplify:whale_alert:11` was added to `public_identifications.csv`, which reaches T065As's living members through their matriline, T065s as a sub-lineage's report, and the Bigg's ecotype. Every row of `snapshot_group_occurrences.csv` gained `via_group`, `via_member` and `vitality` (a member's report's own, so T137A's dead record on `inaturalist:9000002` is dead on T137s too), and the rows of a matriline's members' direct claims, T137A and T137B for T137s, T65A and T65A2 for T065s and T065As, are new. All of these are the build's answer, checked by hand.

Maplify names a sighting by its source as well as its id (decision 112), so every `maplify:<id>` in the snapshot became `maplify:<source>:<id>`: `maplify:whale_alert:11`, `maplify:acartia:13`, and so on, the source being the one in `maplify_sightings.csv`. Postgres never named them so; the new ids are the build's answer, and a substitution of the old ones, checked by hand. `dwc_occurrences.csv` carries them as `occurrenceID`.

## When a derivation changes on purpose

The test then fails, naming the rows that differ. With no Postgres to ask, the new answer is the build's: check that the difference is the one you meant, then edit the expected rows in `snapshot/` to match, as a reviewed change.
